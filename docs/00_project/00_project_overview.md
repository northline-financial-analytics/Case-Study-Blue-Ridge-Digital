# 1. Purpose of the Case Study

Blue Ridge Digital is a fictional small business created to demonstrate the financial planning and analysis, financial modeling, forecasting, management reporting, and decision-support services offered by **Northline Financial Analytics**.

The case study is structured as a simulated client engagement rather than as a standalone financial-modeling exercise.

Northline Financial Analytics has been engaged by the management of Blue Ridge Digital to review the company's historical financial and operating performance, identify the primary drivers of profitability and cash flow, develop an integrated financial forecast, evaluate management's growth plans, and provide recommendations regarding the company's financial priorities for 2026.

The engagement is intended to demonstrate the complete analytical process from initial client data through management recommendations:

1. Review client-provided financial and operating data.
2. Validate and reconcile source information.
3. Analyze historical financial performance.
4. Identify significant trends and anomalies.
5. Normalize historical results where appropriate.
6. Identify key revenue, expense, and operating drivers.
7. Develop financial and operational KPIs.
8. Construct an integrated financial model.
9. Develop a 12-month operating forecast.
10. Prepare a rolling 13-week cash-flow forecast.
11. Compare budget expectations with actual historical performance.
12. Develop base, upside, and downside scenarios.
13. Evaluate proposed hiring and investment decisions.
14. Develop executive-level management reporting.
15. Provide written findings and management recommendations.

The objective is not simply to construct a financial model. The objective is to demonstrate how financial and operational information can be transformed into practical management insight.

# 3. Company Background

## 3.1 Company Overview

Blue Ridge Digital, Inc. is a privately held B2B software company headquartered in the southeastern United States.

The company develops cloud-based workflow and project-management software for small and mid-sized professional-service businesses.

Its customers include firms operating in areas such as:

* engineering;
* architecture;
* environmental consulting;
* surveying;
* construction consulting;
* specialty professional services; and
* other project-oriented businesses.

Blue Ridge Digital's software helps customers manage projects, assignments, schedules, internal workflows, document approvals, staff coordination, and management reporting.

The company operates primarily through a recurring subscription model supplemented by implementation, onboarding, training, and other professional-service revenue.

Blue Ridge Digital was founded several years before the historical period included in this case study. By January 2023, the company had moved beyond its initial startup phase and had established a viable product, recurring customer base, and recognizable market niche.

Management's focus during 2023 through 2025 shifted increasingly from product validation toward growth.

# 6. Historical Business Development

The three-year historical period represents an important stage in Blue Ridge Digital's development.

The company experienced meaningful growth between 2023 and 2025, but the relationship between revenue growth, staffing growth, profitability, and cash generation became increasingly complicated.

This ultimately led management to seek outside financial analysis.

---

# 7. Fiscal Year 2023 — Establishing the Growth Platform

At the beginning of 2023, Blue Ridge Digital had established a viable software platform and a stable base of recurring customers.

The company entered the year with approximately:

* 80 active customers;
* 11 employees; and
* annual revenue in the general range of $1.3 million.

Management's primary objectives were to:

* expand the customer base;
* improve the product;
* strengthen customer retention;
* establish a repeatable sales process; and
* build the operational foundation necessary for future growth.

Revenue increased during the year as new customers were added and existing customers expanded their use of the platform.

Payroll remained the company's largest expense, but staffing levels were relatively aligned with the company's size.

Blue Ridge Digital remained profitable and maintained an adequate cash position.

Management viewed 2023 as evidence that the company's business model could support substantially greater scale.

That conclusion influenced decisions made during the following two years.

---

# 8. Fiscal Year 2024 — Accelerating Growth

During 2024, Blue Ridge Digital experienced stronger customer acquisition and revenue growth.

Annual revenue increased to approximately $1.8 million.

The customer base expanded to approximately 115 active customers.

Management interpreted this growth as confirmation that demand for the company's platform was accelerating.

The company responded by increasing investment in several areas.

Additional employees were hired in:

* software development;
* customer success;
* implementation;
* sales; and
* marketing.

Total headcount increased from approximately 11 employees to approximately 15 employees during the year.

Management also increased spending on:

* marketing;
* cloud infrastructure;
* software systems;
* sales tools; and
* customer-support capabilities.

These investments were intended to support continued growth during 2025 and beyond.

Revenue growth initially absorbed much of the additional cost, and management remained optimistic about the company's trajectory.

However, the company's fixed-cost base was becoming substantially larger.

---

# 9. Fiscal Year 2025 — Growth Continues but Financial Pressure Emerges

Blue Ridge Digital entered 2025 expecting another year of strong growth.

Management continued investing in personnel and infrastructure based partly on the assumption that customer acquisition would remain near 2024 levels.

Headcount increased substantially and reached approximately 21 employees during 2025.

However, revenue growth began to moderate.

Annual revenue increased to approximately $2.1 million, representing continued growth but a significant slowdown from management's expectations.

The customer base reached approximately 130 active customers.

Several factors contributed to the slower growth.

These included:

* longer sales cycles;
* increased competition;
* slower conversion of qualified leads;
* customer churn;
* delayed implementation of several new accounts; and
* weaker-than-expected productivity from recent sales hires.

At the same time, operating expenses continued increasing.

Payroll grew considerably faster than revenue as employees hired during late 2024 and 2025 contributed a full or partial year of expense.

Sales and marketing spending also remained elevated.

Technology and software costs increased as the company's infrastructure and internal systems expanded.

The result was an emerging divergence between:

**Revenue growth**

and

**expense growth.**

Although Blue Ridge Digital remained operationally viable and continued generating substantial recurring revenue, profitability weakened.

More importantly, management noticed that the company's cash balance was declining despite continued revenue growth.

This created concern among the company's owners and management team.

---

# 10. The Management Problem

By the end of 2025, Blue Ridge Digital appeared successful when viewed only through the lens of revenue growth.

Revenue had increased substantially over the previous three years.

The customer base had expanded.

The company had developed a larger organization and more sophisticated operating capabilities.

However, management was increasingly concerned about several financial trends.

## 10.1 Declining Profitability

Operating expenses, particularly payroll, had grown faster than revenue.

As a result, operating margins and EBITDA margins had declined.

Management did not have a clear understanding of whether this represented:

* a temporary investment period;
* structural overstaffing;
* insufficient pricing;
* weak sales productivity;
* excessive overhead;
* customer-retention problems; or
* some combination of these factors.

---

## 10.2 Declining Cash Balance

Management observed that cash declined during portions of 2025 even though the company remained profitable on an accrual basis.

The causes were not immediately clear.

Potential contributors included:

* increased payroll;
* slower customer collections;
* changes in accounts receivable;
* timing of annual expenses;
* capital expenditures;
* prepaid expenses;
* customer-payment timing;
* one-time expenses; and
* differences between accounting profit and operating cash flow.

Management wants to understand where cash is being consumed and whether the current trajectory creates a liquidity risk during 2026.

---

# 11. Proposed 2026 Growth Plan

Despite the financial concerns emerging during 2025, management remains optimistic about Blue Ridge Digital's long-term growth prospects.

The sales team believes additional capacity could materially increase customer acquisition.

Management is therefore considering hiring **three additional sales employees during 2026**.

The proposed hires would create several costs:

* base salaries;
* payroll taxes;
* benefits;
* commissions;
* recruiting and onboarding expenses;
* sales software licenses;
* travel and marketing support; and
* management time.

The employees would also require time to reach full productivity.

Management believes the investment could accelerate revenue growth, but it does not currently have a reliable financial model demonstrating:

* how many additional customers must be acquired;
* how quickly the employees must become productive;
* how much incremental recurring revenue must be generated;
* when the investment reaches break-even;
* how much cash is required before break-even;
* how sensitive the decision is to customer churn;
* how the investment affects EBITDA;
* how the investment affects year-end cash; or
* whether the company can safely fund the expansion from existing resources.

This proposed hiring decision becomes one of the central management questions addressed by the case study.

# 12. Additional Historical Issues

The historical data contains several items requiring investigation and interpretation.

These are intentionally incorporated into the case study to reflect the type of analysis required during an actual FP&A engagement.

## 12.1 Expense Classification Change

During the historical period, management changed the accounting classification of selected technology expenses.

Certain expenses previously classified within general and administrative expenses were subsequently classified as cost of revenue or technology expense.

Historical comparisons therefore require normalization to ensure that apparent changes in margins are not simply the result of accounting reclassification.

---

## 12.2 One-Time Employee Bonus

Blue Ridge Digital paid a significant one-time employee bonus during 2025 following completion of a major product release.

The bonus increased reported payroll expense but is not expected to recur at the same level during 2026.

Management wants both reported and normalized profitability to be evaluated.

---

## 12.3 One-Time Professional Expense

The company incurred an unusual legal or professional-services expense during 2025 associated with a specific business matter.

The expenditure is not considered part of normal recurring operations.

The expense should therefore be identified separately when evaluating normalized operating performance.

---

## 12.4 Major Customer Loss

A relatively large customer terminated its subscription during 2025.

The customer represented a meaningful amount of recurring revenue.

The loss affected:

* monthly recurring revenue;
* customer churn;
* annual recurring revenue;
* revenue growth; and
* customer concentration.

The event provides an opportunity to evaluate whether management is adequately monitoring customer retention and concentration risk.

---

## 12.5 Accounts Receivable Deterioration

Accounts receivable increased during 2025.

Several customers paid invoices more slowly than expected.

As a result, reported revenue did not translate immediately into cash receipts.

This contributed to the disconnect between accounting profitability and the company's cash position.

The case study will evaluate:

* accounts receivable trends;
* days sales outstanding;
* collections performance;
* customer-payment timing; and
* the impact of working capital on cash flow.

---

## 12.6 Annual and Irregular Expenses

Several expenses are paid annually or at irregular intervals rather than evenly throughout the year.

Examples include:

* insurance;
* selected software subscriptions;
* professional memberships;
* annual technology contracts; and
* other prepaid services.

These payments create monthly cash-flow fluctuations that are not immediately apparent from annual financial statements.

They are particularly important when developing the 13-week cash-flow forecast.

# 29. Case Study Philosophy

This case study is intentionally designed to extend beyond mechanical financial modeling.

Blue Ridge Digital represents a business that is growing but beginning to encounter the financial complexity that often accompanies growth.

Revenue alone does not determine financial health.

A company can increase revenue while simultaneously experiencing:

* declining margins;
* deteriorating working capital;
* excessive hiring;
* weaker productivity;
* customer-retention problems; and
* declining liquidity.

The role of financial planning and analysis is to connect these individual signals into a coherent understanding of the business.

For that reason, the Blue Ridge Digital case study follows the progression:

**Data → Information → Analysis → Insight → Decision**

The financial model is a tool within that process.

The ultimate deliverable is better management decision-making.

# 31. Initial Engagement Position

At the beginning of the engagement, Northline Financial Analytics has **not yet reached a conclusion** regarding Blue Ridge Digital's financial condition or management's proposed growth plan.

The facts presented in this project overview establish the business context and known management concerns. They do not predetermine the analytical conclusion.

The financial analysis should determine whether management's concerns are supported by the data and whether additional issues emerge during the engagement.

In particular, the case study should not assume in advance that:

* the company is overstaffed;
* the proposed sales hires should be rejected;
* declining cash necessarily represents financial distress;
* customer churn is excessive;
* management's budget is unrealistic; or
* any particular corrective action is required.

Those conclusions must be supported by the financial and operating analysis.

This distinction is fundamental to the case study.

**The scenario establishes the questions. The analysis determines the answers.**
