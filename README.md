# Blue Ridge Digital

## Financial Planning & Analysis Case Study

**Northline Financial Analytics**

Blue Ridge Digital is a fictional company developed as a comprehensive financial planning and analysis (FP&A) case study for **Northline Financial Analytics**.

The case study demonstrates the development of an integrated financial model from historical financial statements through forecasting, cash-flow planning, scenario analysis, management reporting, and decision support.

> **Note:** Blue Ridge Digital is a fictional company. All company information, financial statements, operating data, assumptions, and results contained in this case study are simulated for educational and portfolio purposes. No real client or confidential financial information is used.

---

## Case Study Objective

The objective of this case study is to demonstrate how financial and operational data can be transformed into a practical decision-support system for management.

The completed analysis is designed to help management answer questions such as:

* How is the business performing financially?
* What are the primary drivers of revenue, expenses, and profitability?
* How is performance changing over time?
* What does the next 12 months look like under current assumptions?
* How much cash does the business need to operate safely?
* What happens if revenue growth slows or expenses increase?
* Can the company afford additional hiring or investment?
* Which financial and operating metrics should management monitor?
* What actions should management consider based on the analysis?

The goal is not simply to produce financial statements or forecasts, but to convert financial information into **actionable management insight**.

# Scope of Analysis

The completed Blue Ridge Digital case study will include the following components.

## 1. Historical Financial Analysis

Three years of historical financial statements will be analyzed to establish the company's financial baseline.

Analysis will include:

* Income statement
* Balance sheet
* Cash-flow statement
* Common-size analysis
* Year-over-year trend analysis
* Financial ratios
* Key performance indicators (KPIs)

The historical analysis will identify important trends in revenue, expenses, profitability, liquidity, and financial position.

---

## 2. Revenue and Expense Drivers

The model will identify the operating assumptions that drive financial performance.

Potential drivers include:

* Customer growth
* Customer retention
* Pricing
* Average revenue per customer
* Payroll and staffing
* Software and technology expenses
* Sales and marketing expenses
* General and administrative expenses

Separating operating drivers from financial outputs allows management to evaluate how changes in the business affect future financial performance.

---

## 3. 12-Month Operating Forecast

A monthly operating forecast will project the company's expected financial performance over the next 12 months.

The forecast will incorporate assumptions related to:

* Revenue growth
* Customer activity
* Pricing
* Staffing
* Operating expenses
* Capital expenditures
* Working capital

Actual performance can then be compared with forecast expectations as new results become available.

---

## 4. Integrated Three-Statement Financial Model

The case study will include an integrated financial model connecting the:

* Income statement
* Balance sheet
* Cash-flow statement

Changes to operating assumptions will flow through the model so that their effects on profitability, financial position, and cash can be evaluated together.

---

## 5. 13-Week Cash-Flow Forecast

A rolling 13-week cash-flow forecast will provide a short-term view of liquidity.

The forecast will evaluate:

* Beginning cash
* Expected cash receipts
* Payroll
* Operating expenses
* Capital expenditures
* Other cash requirements
* Ending cash
* Minimum cash requirements

This provides management with an early-warning system for potential liquidity constraints.

---

## 6. Budget vs. Actual Analysis

Actual financial performance will be compared with budget or forecast expectations.

Variance analysis will identify:

* Revenue variances
* Expense variances
* Profitability variances
* Material deviations from expectations

The purpose is not merely to identify that a variance occurred, but to understand **why it occurred and whether management action is warranted**.

---

## 7. Scenario Analysis

The financial model will evaluate multiple operating scenarios.

### Base Case

Represents management's expected operating environment.

### Upside Case

Evaluates stronger-than-expected operating performance.

### Downside Case

Evaluates the financial impact of weaker revenue, higher costs, or other adverse conditions.

Scenario analysis allows management to understand both potential opportunities and financial risks before decisions are made.

---

## 8. Hiring and Investment Decision Analysis

The model will evaluate selected management decisions, such as:

* Adding employees
* Increasing sales or marketing spending
* Purchasing equipment or technology
* Making other significant operating investments

The analysis will consider the impact of these decisions on:

* Profitability
* Cash flow
* Break-even timing
* Financial capacity
* Expected return

---

## 9. Executive Dashboard

An executive dashboard will summarize the company's most important financial and operating information.

Potential dashboard metrics include:

* Revenue
* Revenue growth
* Gross margin
* Operating expenses
* EBITDA
* Net income
* Operating cash flow
* Cash balance
* Cash runway
* Budget variance
* Selected operating KPIs

The dashboard is intended to provide management with a concise view of business performance without requiring executives to navigate the underlying financial model.

---

## 10. Management Recommendation

The case study will conclude with a written management assessment summarizing:

* Key findings
* Financial strengths
* Financial risks
* Important operating trends
* Forecast implications
* Scenario results
* Recommended management actions

The final deliverable will demonstrate how financial modeling can support practical business decisions rather than functioning solely as a reporting exercise.
